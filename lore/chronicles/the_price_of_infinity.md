# The PTC Chronicles
## The Price of Infinity

*From the historical archives of humanity.*

In the year 2243, humanity finally mastered controlled fusion and discovered a means by which its extraordinary energy could be stored.

The age of energy scarcity was over.

A single domestic fusion unit could provide a household with a virtually inexhaustible supply of energy, requiring little more than seawater and a small quantity of consumable chemical compounds.

For a brief moment, there was hope.

Some believed humanity had finally discovered the means to free itself. No longer would nations fight over dwindling reserves of fuel. No longer would ordinary people surrender the greater part of their lives simply to afford the energy necessary to survive.

For the first time in human history, abundance appeared within reach of everyone.

The arrogance.

The hubris.

The technocratic oligarchs moved quickly. Through corruption, political influence, and the acquisition of competing technologies, they established monopolies over the very processes that might have liberated humanity.

The production of energy without authorization from Central became a criminal offense. Those caught operating unlicensed fusion systems could spend the remainder of their lives laboring in mining colonies on the other side of the galaxy.

Humanity had discovered nearly limitless power.

And what do powerful men desire?

More power.

### The race to the stars

A new race to the stars began shortly after the discovery.

Vessels could operate for centuries without refueling, traveling at speeds previously thought impossible. Combat aircraft could roam the depths of space for days, weeks, or even months without returning to their carriers, striking from virtually anywhere at any time.

Artificial intelligence accelerated scientific discovery at an unprecedented rate. Generations of research could be compressed into years, sometimes months.

Humanity expanded beyond its solar system, establishing colonies deeper and deeper into the galaxy.

And then we discovered that we were not alone.

Where peace was offered, we chose war.

Where knowledge was offered, we sought domination.

Where others offered to share, we decided to take everything by force.

### The First Intergalactic War

They say the arc of the moral universe is long, but it bends toward justice.

How painfully true those words would prove to be.

No sooner had humanity discovered a nearly inexhaustible source of energy than it began to consume the very world that had made that discovery possible.

As the war expanded, so too did the demands of the machinery that sustained it.

Fusion required fuel.

Fuel required water.

And the war machine was insatiable.

Water, once considered abundant on Earth, proved remarkably scarce throughout much of the known galaxy. Few inhabited worlds possessed oceans of their own. Many extracted their water from beneath the surface, from frozen bodies, or from distant celestial objects.

Some civilizations had already lost their oceans pursuing ambitions as futile as our own.

Earth, with its vast reserves of seawater, became one of the principal sources of fuel for humanity's expanding military empire.

Within a few centuries of the outbreak of the First Intergalactic War, Earth's oceans began to recede.

What had once been considered an inexhaustible resource was being consumed on a planetary scale.

The seas diminished. Ecosystems collapsed. The climate changed. Entire regions became uninhabitable.

Still, the war continued.

Humanity had learned how to harness the power of the stars, yet had failed to learn when enough was enough.

### The downfall

Eventually, the civilizations humanity had sought to conquer united against it.

The empire fractured.

Its fleets were destroyed, its colonies isolated, and Earth itself was brought to the brink of annihilation.

Nuclear, chemical, and biological warfare completed much of the devastation that centuries of environmental exploitation had already begun.

Humanity survived, but only as a scattered remnant of what it had once been.

Among the survivors were colonies that had never supported the war. Some had deliberately withdrawn from the expanding empire, seeking distant worlds where they could live without conquest, without the ambitions of Central, and without the endless pursuit of power.

Places where one could grow a garden.

Raise a family.

Live at peace with others.

And perhaps, finally, learn from the mistakes of those who came before.

### The return

Nearly a millennium has passed.

The exact year is uncertain. Surviving historical records are incomplete, and the calendars maintained by humanity's scattered colonies no longer agree.

By the old terrestrial calendar, it is believed to be sometime in the fourth millennium.

Earth has become little more than a memory.

A place remembered through fragmented archives, ancient recordings, and stories passed from one generation to the next.

But now, humanity has returned.

Commander Kara Voss is part of a forward operation tasked with determining whether life on humanity's ancestral world remains possible, whether its environment can be restored, and whether Earth might once again become a place where people can live.

Those questions will have to wait.

Her aircraft lies broken in the desert.

Her equipment is limited.

And the world she has returned to is not quite as abandoned as she had been led to believe.

For now, Kara has a considerably more immediate objective.

Get her baby back in the barn.

The science can wait.
