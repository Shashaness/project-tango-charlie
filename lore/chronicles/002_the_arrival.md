# Chapter II — The Arrival

TC167 was coming apart.

Warning indicators flooded Kara's displays. Flight control systems were failing faster than she could compensate. The violent atmospheric entry had torn through sections of the aircraft's exterior, and what remained of its propulsion system was struggling to keep her airborne.

Through the dust and atmospheric haze, she saw something.

A city.

An immense collection of structures stretched across the horizon. Even from this distance, she could make out the silhouettes of towers rising above the barren landscape.

She adjusted her heading.

If she could reach the city, there might be shelter. There might be equipment. There might be something left worth salvaging.

Then she spotted a long, unmistakably artificial strip of land.

A runway.

An airport.

She fought the controls, trying to bring TC167 closer. The aircraft shuddered violently. Another system failed. The ground was approaching far too quickly.

She tried to pull up.

Then everything went black.

---

When Kara opened her eyes, there was silence.

For a moment, she couldn't remember where she was.

Pain slowly returned to her body. Dust hung in the cockpit. Most of the instruments were dark, and the few that remained illuminated offered little reassurance.

She released her harness and struggled out of the wreckage.

TC167 lay broken against the unforgiving surface of Earth. Sections of its exterior had been torn away. Its propulsion systems were silent. The aircraft that had carried her across the final distance to humanity's ancestral home was barely recognizable.

She wasn't sure it would ever fly again.

In the distance, beyond the wreckage, stood the airport she had seen during her descent.

Several buildings remained standing. Among them was what appeared to be a large aircraft hangar.

Beyond the airport, the ruins of the city loomed against the horizon.

Kara looked back at TC167.

She had no idea how she was going to move it.

She had no idea whether the equipment necessary to repair it still existed.

But she knew where she needed to start.

She began walking toward the hangar.
